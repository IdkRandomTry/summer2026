Content Provenance attempts to give verifiable information regarding the authenticity of a media, often involving its source/origin and potentially involving the edits which were made.
# Motivation
With the increase in quality of AI generated images, it has become harder to distinguish between real (organic) images captured by cameras and those generated by synthetic means. This has resulted in a decrease in people's trust in the media published on the internet. To regain the trust a system must be designed (and implemented) which allows users to verify the validity/source of the image. This is also of utmost importance when images from the internet are used in the court of law to make a judgement.

> [!Idea] Digital Signatures
> Digital Signatures appear to be solving a similar problem for a different medium. Digital signatures are a mathematical scheme for verifying the authenticity of digital messages or documents. Popular digital signatures schemes rely on cryptographically generated "key pairs" of *private/secret key ($sk$)* and *public key ($pk$)* . These key pairs have certain mathematical properties which allow anyone to use the $pk$ to verify that a *public message* is *signed* by a particular $sk$ i.e. endorsed by the holder of that $sk$. For more on this tangent check out [[Digital Signature Schemes]].

The nature of the problems calls for using cryptographic techniques of similar nature. Infact the most straightforward solution involves adding software to cameras which sign the raw image as soon as captured! However, notice that when an image is altered the sign will no longer hold. It is common for legitimate photographs to undergo edits before publishing which makes the signature void.
