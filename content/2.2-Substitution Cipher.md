### Idea
The Substitution Cipher is a method where each letter in the plaintext alphabet is replaced by a corresponding letter from a shuffled version of the alphabet. Unlike a simple shift, the key is a complete permutation of the 26 letters, allowing for $26!$ possible mappings. To encrypt, you replace each character with its mapped counterpart; to decrypt, you apply the inverse of that mapping.


### Example
**Key (Random Permutation):**
`A->Q, B->W, C->E, D->R, ...`

* **Plaintext:** `BAD`
* **Encryption:**
    * `B` maps to `W`
    * `A` maps to `Q`
    * `D` maps to `R`
* **Ciphertext:** `WQR`

### Formally
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{M} &= \{A, B, \dots, Z\}^* \\
\mathcal{K} &= \text{the set of permutations of } \{A, B, \dots, Z\} \\
\text{Gen} &= k \text{ where } k \xleftarrow{r} \mathcal{K} \\
\text{Enc}_k(m_1 \dots m_n) &= c_1 \dots c_n \text{ where } c_i = k(m_i) \\
\text{Dec}_k(c_1 c_2 \dots c_n) &= m_1 m_2 \dots m_n \text{ where } m_i = k^{-1}(c_i)
\end{aligned}
$$

### Vulnerabilities
The cipher is subject to **frequency attacks** which take advantage of the difference in frequency of letters appearing in spoken language.
