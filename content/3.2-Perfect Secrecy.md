> [!Idea]
> The distribution of ciphertext for any 2 messages are identical


($\mathcal{M}$, $\mathcal{K}$, `Gen`, `Enc`, `Dec`) is a private key encryption. It is said to be *perfectly secret* if for all $m_1$ and $m_2$ in $\mathcal{M}$ and for all ciphertext $c$ and key $k$ :
$$Pr[Enc_k(m_1) = c] = Pr[Enc_k(m_2) = c]$$

This is Equivalent to [[3.1-Shannon's Secrecy]] as shown in [[3-Modern Cryptography#Theorem A private-key encryption scheme is perfectly secret if and only if it is Shannon secret.|Theorem]]
